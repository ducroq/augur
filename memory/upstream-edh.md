## Division of labour — read this before trusting either side's alarm

**Augur's `t0` hold-back and heartbeat detect the ABSENCE of a fresh vintage;
EDH's issue explains a run that fired and FAILED.** Ours covers the worse case.

⚠️ EDH's publish-failure alerting is **DIAGNOSIS, NOT DETECTION. Do not make it
the primary alarm.** (An earlier note had this hierarchy backwards.) Its `alert:`
job lives *inside* `energydatahub/.github/workflows/collect-data.yml` with
`needs: [collect-and-publish, deploy]`, and that workflow's only triggers are
`schedule` and `workflow_dispatch` — verified on main. It is therefore
structurally blind to a run that never starts: a dropped cron (GitHub
deprioritises schedules on quiet repos), a queue outage, or a disabled workflow
all produce no run, no alert, and a clean issue tracker while nothing publishes.

⚠️ **Our alarm firing with NO issue on `ducroq/energydatahub` is a WORSE signal
than an issue existing** — it means the pipeline is not running at all, rather
than running and failing. For the 08-31..09-03 outage theirs would have fired on
all five days; had the cron simply stopped, theirs would have been silent on all
five and only ours would have caught it. A true liveness check must fire when a
publish has *not* happened for N hours and cannot live inside the workflow it
watches — unbuilt on both sides, and the same shape as Augur's own accepted blind
spot (the heartbeat runs *on* sadalsuud, so it cannot report sadalsuud down).

## Publish outage 2026-08-31..09-04 — five vintages lost, upstream, resolved

The tripwire (`energydatahub/.github/workflows/collect-data.yml:153`) runs *before* the `Update energy data` commit (`:183`) and compares against `--previous-ref HEAD`, but `data/_shape_signatures.json` is only written *by* that commit — so the baseline froze at 08-30 and the gate was guaranteed to re-fire, with a different offending feed each run as the gap widened. Only `Record shape observation` commits landed, which made the repo history look alive while publishing nothing. **Augur behaved correctly throughout**: the t0 guard, the hold-back alarm and the heartbeat all fired, the heartbeat emailed on day 1 and then deduplicated by shape, and on 09-04 the new content-contract gate **refused a pre-auction vintage** (dispatched 06:53 UTC, `entsoe` at 96 points vs a 192 median — the day-ahead auction clears ~12:00 CET). No corrupt data entered the parquet. **The fix is EDH-side and was theirs to make.** A time-boxed drift block (block at most 3 consecutive runs, then relent, baseline still only advancing on a pass) was implemented and then **abandoned, not shipped** — their review found its only reachable domain was `CRITICAL_FEEDS` + `MEMBER_MAPPED_FEEDS`, the two sets that must never be waved through, and that it would have relented on **zero of the five** runs this outage actually produced. ## `load_forecast` horizon halved 2026-08-28, recovered 2026-09-04 — NOT structural

decrypted every `load_forecast` vintage on EDH `origin/main` and counted **distinct calendar days** as well as points, so a resolution change cannot be mistaken for a horizon change. Normal is **192 pts / 2 days** for months through 08-26; **96 pts / 1 day** on 08-28, 08-29, 08-30 and 09-04. `metadata.end_time` still declares +2 days throughout, so the envelope over-declares its own coverage and EDH's request window is unchanged — this is **upstream ENTSO-E A65 availability**. **RESOLVED 2026-09-04 on the load side — both zones recovered; see `docs/hypothesis-log.md` for the full table.** EDH's `260904_081356` publish (08:14 UTC) carries **load NL 192/2 days AND DE_LU 192/2 days**. A direct A65 probe 15 minutes earlier (09:59 CEST) had shown DE_LU still at 96, which **confirms the publication-timing effect** — German day-ahead load lands later in the day than Dutch, so a morning DE_LU short reading is benign. The 08-28..08-30 episode was nonetheless a **genuine both-zone A65 gap**, not timing: `260830_190255` was short at 19:02, far later than today's ~10:14 recovery. Net: a real multi-day upstream gap that healed, costing three vintages of reach — so **energydatahub#51 closes as resolved-upstream, not as transient**, and the "new steady state" reading is falsified *before its pre-committed signal could accumulate* (it needed load to stay at 96; load is back at 192). ⚠️ Price at 96 in both of today's publishes is **not** the divergence — both are pre-auction (the NL day-ahead clears ~12:00 CET). **This is an NL-only question for Augur**: `consolidate.py:320 parse_load_file` reads `load_data.get("NL", {})` hard-coded, and `grep -rn "DE_LU" ml/ static/js/ scripts/` returns zero hits repo-wide — DE_LU is structurally invisible here, so do not spend time on the German half. ⚠️ **Two wrong readings to not repeat, both held during the 09-04 session**: "load_forecast is a same-day feed, 96 quarter-hours by construction, so the 18h hold-back is permanent and should be designed around" (refuted — it carried 192 for months), and the mirror error of generalising a structural shape from a *single* vintage. The discriminator both readings missed is **publish hour**: every pre-08-28 short vintage is an early-hour run that recovered to 192 on the same day's normal-hour run (08-09, 08-24), so short-at-early-hour is benign and normal-hour-short is the signal. **`260830_190255` at 19:02 is the lone unambiguous normal-hour short vintage**, which is why the feed-divergence hypothesis's Alternative 1 ("new steady state", signal = 3 consecutive normal-hour publishes at price 192 / load 96) is **strongly indicated but formally short** — the outage denied us the normal-hour publishes the test needs. Resolve on the next two scheduled EDH runs. **energydatahub#51 must not be closed as transient on this evidence.** Standing gap, told to EDH and unowned by either side: `load_forecast` is in their `CRITICAL_FEEDS` and delivered half its horizon for four publishes without their gate objecting, because **a shape signature captures structure, not span** — 96 and 192 records have identical shape. Augur's t0 hold-back alarm was the only thing that noticed.
